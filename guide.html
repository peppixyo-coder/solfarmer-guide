1. What SolFarmer actually is

A browser game — no download, no wallet needed to start. You sign up, receive a free Basic Validator, place it in an underground cavern, and it mines a share of a SOL pool whether the tab is open or not. Around that core: a shop of 48 collectible rigs in four rarity tiers (Common, Rare, Epic, Legendary), a merge system, a story campaign in chapters, daily quests, a daily lucky chest, an arcade of 8 mini-games, guild wars, mining pools, a player market, and a separate expedition-and-PvP layer called the Camp.

It pays out in real SOL, on-chain, verifiable on Solscan. That part is not a scam. The part that hurts people is that the game is pay-to-win by design and the whole economy is built around it — every SOL you deposit converts into a non-withdrawable internal currency. Treat it as a game you might spend money on, not as an income.

The one-sentence version. SolFarmer is "Cookie Clicker with real money": a genuinely charming idle game wrapped around a real-SOL payout system that runs, in part, on other players' deposits.

2. SOL vs SSOL — the economy change nobody saw coming

In November 2026 SolFarmer added a second currency, SSOL, and it changed everything about how a new player should approach the game. The official explanation says SSOL exists to deter bots, push players toward mining, keep market prices stable, and enable automated withdrawals. The player-side reality is simpler and harsher:

  • Deposited SOL converts to SSOL, and SSOL cannot be withdrawn or converted back into SOL. The conversion is one-way, in steps as small as 0.001 SOL (in the game files).
  • The only withdrawable SOL now comes from mining and PvP season rewards. Quests, lucky chests, arcade prizes and marketplace sales are paid in SSOL — even where the marketing copy still says "real SOL".
  • Before the change, market sales paid out real SOL. Players who focused on the Camp and trading full-time found their bankroll locked: the only way back out is buying miners and mining it out — 8–9 months by player estimates.
  • The change landed with about three days of notice. The official documentation used to read that assets may be withdrawn at any time.

Decide before your first deposit: deposited SOL is spent money, not an investment you can pull back out. Mine with it, play with it, never count on seeing it again in SOL form. The mining itself still pays real, withdrawable SOL — that part did not change.

3. The free daily loop, in order

Idle games are won by routines, not by sessions. The developers hand the free part out generously — this is the order that captures all of it in about three minutes a day.

  1. Claim your mined balance first. The client computes your pending earnings as the minutes elapsed since your last claim, capped at 480 minutes (8 hours). Claim every 8 hours or you are only collecting a fraction of what your rigs supposedly made — three claims a day covers all 24 hours, one claim a day covers a third of it.
  2. Daily check-in / streak. Rewards escalate each consecutive day; the day-7 payout is roughly 7× the day-1 one. Break the streak and it resets to nothing.
  3. Free lucky chest. One chest every 24 hours, guaranteed reward inside, with paid tiers from Bronze up to Cosmic. The free one pays around 0.0001–0.0005 SOL (in the game files). One click. Never skip it.
  4. Bounty Board (daily quests). A few objectives like "merge miners" or "play one arcade game" — the client ships with 3 per day by default (in the game files). Thirty seconds each, paid in currency and XP.
  5. Daily Arcade. Eight retro mini-games (Snake, Hash Slots, Runner and friends), one play per game per UTC day — no grinding trap. High scores pay out and the top of the daily table takes about 30% of the prize pool. Every point you score during a guild war also counts for your team.
  6. Social hub and friend visits. Visit other farms and leave a "like": you earn a small tip, and players who visit your farm pay you back. Going first creates the reciprocity that fills your own claimable social balance. The Tavern hub and named companion characters live in the same section.
  7. Story quests / chapters. The campaign pays out as you discover new chapters — Chapter VI: "Mastery" is the latest, deployed June 23, 2026. Claim everything completed before you log off.
Do not chase the arcade leaderboard. Top daily scores run into the tens of thousands of points and are scripted runs, not human play. Treat the arcade as a small fixed daily income and the top cut as out of reach.

4. Rack slots, power and merging: the real gameplay

Your starting room has 12 slots and a 2,000 W power budget. Slots and watts — not money — are the binding constraints: fill the 12 slots and the farm stops growing no matter how much you spend, and a rig that draws more than the room allows cannot be placed at all. Every rig in the shop has a watt draw, and that number is a room capacity stat, not a real electricity bill: the client ships an electricityCostPerWatt constant in its economy settings, but nothing in the client charges it against your SOL balance. Treat watts as space, not as a cost.

  • Merge to compress. Two identical miners at the same level fuse into one miner of the next level. 16 level-1 miners become one level-5 miner, and that level-5 does the work of 16 for the space and the power draw of a single rig. This is the core mechanic of the entire game.
  • Merges can pay out extra. A successful merge can roll a bonus hash-rate percentage (2.7% on one observed merge). The client ships with an 80% merge success rate (in the game files) — expect roughly one merge in five to fail and budget for it.
  • The merge fee is quoted, not calculated. Merging is priced server-side: the client sends the pair and receives back the new level, the new hash rate and the exact fee, so the number you see in the confirmation dialog is the real one. Its shipped fallback settings contain two different models (a base of 0.001 multiplied by ~1.8 per level, and tiered fees of 0.1 / 0.15 / 0.2 for L1→2, L3→4 and L5+), which is exactly why you should read the dialog instead of trusting a formula from a guide — including this one.
  • Merging never increases power draw. Compare rigs by hash rate per slot and per watt, not by purchase price.
  • Stacking bonuses for same-rarity or same-miner groups in one room are negligible until you have high-rarity racks. Raw hash rate wins the first two weeks, every time.
Coming back to a full rack? Merge everything that can be merged before buying anything new. A merge hands back slots, gives free XP, and needs no new power capacity. This one habit unsticks almost every stalled account.

5. XP and the fastest way to level

There are three XP sources, all on the rack: buying a miner, placing it, and merging. Buying 25, placing 15, merging 20 gives roughly 100 XP per pair of starter miners — which is why the whole leveling routine is: buy pairs of the cheapest miner, place both, merge them, repeat.

Level requirements scale linearly, not exponentially — confirmed by the game's top earners — so a free player can grind for months without the wall ever becoming insurmountable. Two consequences:

  1. The level 9 wall is real but finite. One observed run had ~6,400 XP available passively where ~8,100 were needed — a gap no amount of waiting closes. Through it: keep merging as small currency batches arrive (free, slow — this is the design), or make a small deposit (fast — this is exactly the moment the game wants you to hit).
  2. Levels keep unlocking real content: new shop tiers at 11, 15, 18, 24, 25, 32… The Forge (level 10) burns 3 miners to return a rarer variant, and the Camp (level 15) opens the whole second game inside the game — see section 7.

Leveling itself is not the point — hashrate is. Every level is only worth what it unlocks: a cheaper slot per unit of hashrate, or the next rung of the buy ladder below.

7. Level 15: The Camp, classes and PvP

The Camp is a second game inside the game, and the reason most long-term players stay. You pick a character, spend its skill points (3 per level), and do two things with it:

  • Expeditions. Send your character across biomes — crystal forests, lava pits, icy peaks — to loot gear. Expeditions are timer-based and luck-driven: Luck raises the chance of higher-tier drops, up to Cosmic. Looted gear can be equipped, forged into mythic variants, or sold on the marketplace.
  • PvP. Turn-based, offline: your character fights other players' characters automatically. Attack against defence, skills against skills, best gear wins — victory is essentially decided by stats and gear before the fight plays out. Seasons run roughly every two weeks and pay out the top of the ladder.

The honest caveats. Camp income used to be real SOL from gear sales; after the SSOL change, loot sells for SSOL — a fire sale at the time of writing. And the top of the ladder is not a fair fight: serious guilds coordinate win-trading and gear-swapping between accounts, and one player documented a single guild member holding ~26% of the traded supply of over 500 items. Compete for fun and for the season prizes, not as an income.

How the fights actually resolve (player-reported)

None of this is in the client — it comes from players who have run several seasons, so treat it as informed hearsay rather than a spec sheet:

  • Damage first, then health. A fight is decided by total damage dealt, then by who has more HP left when the damage is totalled — not by who has the biggest power rating. That is why a lower-rated character can beat a higher-rated one, and why the ranking feels random until you learn the build.
  • Luck drives crits. The working figure players quote is 5 luck = 1% crit chance, with a base of roughly 35 luck on a fresh character. Reaching 50 luck for an extra 10% crit is a lot of levels, which is why gear does most of the work.
  • Your build is locked for the season. One class, one stat spread, no respec until the season ends — and seasons run about two weeks (three for the current one). A bad choice costs you a whole season, so copy nothing blindly: nobody, including the people making videos, has enough seasons of data to know what is optimal.
  • Skills stack on different turns. Openers like Light Hit (acts first, +10% crit) and Crit Stance (+35% crit bonus) look like they combine; they do not fire on the same turn. Build the rotation, not the stat sheet.
  • Gear vocabulary, in the order it matters: blueprints add +1/+2/+3 levels to a piece, recipes raise its rarity, scrolls upgrade it, fragments are crafting material, and enchanting adds +1 per enchant. Rarity raises both the size of the bonuses and their roll chance, so a high-rarity base with bad rolls can be worse than a mid-rarity one with good rolls.
  • Expedition upgrades do three separate things: raise gear drop chance, add extra expedition runs, and shorten expedition timers. Run short expeditions while you are awake and a long one overnight — downtime is the only thing you cannot buy back.
Free-to-play priority: The Camp is the only path to good gear and meaningful currency that does not pass through the deposit button. Level 15 is the single biggest unlock in the game — get there before you spend anything.

8. Guilds, guild wars and mining pools

  • Creating a guild costs 10 SOL (players report the price drops to ~2.5 at times); joining is free if a guild accepts you.
  • Guild wars are a 24-hour hashrate duel against a rival guild. Members stake SSOL into a pot, every miner placed and every arcade point scored during the window counts toward the score, and the winning guild splits the pot — one documented pot was 6 SSOL.
  • Mining pools distribute periodic rewards by hashrate contribution, league-tiered; guild membership also passively boosts your own farm's output. The boost is small at first and grows with the guild.
  • Recruitment reality: serious guilds ask for 100+ H/s and, in some cases, proof of activity. Ignore any recruiter asking for a deposit up front — that is not how admission works.

9. The player marketplace

Miners and Camp gear trade between players. The platform takes a 3% fee on sales and allows listing prices from 0.0001 to 100 SOL (in the game files). Two things to know before you list:

  1. Prices are player-set and manipulated. Low-volume items are easy to corner — documented cases include one account controlling ~26% of a 500+ item supply to set the price. Check recent sale history, not just the cheapest listing, before buying anything expensive.
  2. Sales pay SSOL now. Before November 2026 a sale returned withdrawable SOL; today it returns in-game currency. The market is where you convert duplicate rigs into useful ones — not where you cash out.

10. Withdrawing your SOL, step by step

  1. Minimum is 0.1 SOL. Below it, the client refuses the request outright: "Minimum withdrawal is 0.1 SOL."
  2. Install a Solana wallet (Phantom, Solflare, Backpack) if you do not have one. Playing never required a wallet — cashing out does.
  3. Request the withdrawal in-game, then confirm the transaction. It is a deliberate two-step flow: the confirm step is where a wrong address becomes permanent, so verify the first and last characters.
  4. The transfer is on-chain: paste the transaction signature into Solscan to watch it land, usually within seconds.
Do a small test withdrawal first. One reddit user reported having their account banned after withdrawing 0.15 SOL as a test — a single, unverified report, but a cheap insurance policy worth copying: withdraw the minimum once, confirm it arrives, then move larger amounts. And note that the withdrawal security e-mail tends to land in the spam folder — check it there before assuming it never came.

11. Honest earnings: the numbers nobody shows you

Everything above this line was mechanics. This section is what those mechanics produce, from the game's own documentation and from a player who reached the top-5 earnings leaderboard:

  • The game's own estimate for a new free player: 0.01–0.05 SOL in the first week — single-digit dollars at current prices.
  • Paid progression, measured: ~20 SOL spent in the first three days produced roughly 1 SOL per month per 9 SOL invested early; mid-game that improved to about 1 SOL per month per 7 SOL invested.
  • The free tier, measured: "pennies per month" — the top earner's own words. Free play funds itself slowly; it does not pay you.
  • The yield formula, from the client: pending earnings = (your total hash rate / 10,000) × solPerHashrate × minutes since last claim, with the minutes capped at 480 (8 hours). The shipped default is solPerHashrate = 0.00000023 SOL per hash-rate unit per minute — that is roughly 0.00033 SOL per day per H/s at three claims a day, and it is linear: double the hash rate, double the yield. The client fetches these constants from the server at startup, so live values can be different — the calculator has a calibration box for exactly this reason.
  • Whether the rate dilutes as the player base grows is not in the client. Older player write-ups say per-rig income falls as more players join; the client's own accrual preview contains no network-share term. Both can be true if the server keeps adjusting the rate — so treat any "you will earn X per day" claim, including ours, as a snapshot that ages.
  • Deposits are not recoverable. Since the SSOL change, a deposit buys miners that mine back withdrawable SOL over months. That is the deal; read it before you pay.
Verdict. SolFarmer is a well-made idle game with a real payout rail. As entertainment with a possible upside, fine. As an investment, the house odds are terrible and the "investors" are the product. Never deposit money you would not spend on a video game.

12. Test it yourself, in three days

You do not need this guide to vet the game — you need three days and a free account.

  1. Day 1: sign up with no wallet, no deposit. Claim the free chest, play one arcade game, place the starter rig. Note how long it takes to reach level 5.
  2. Day 2: merge everything once, run the daily loop, check the bounty board. Ask yourself: was this fun, or was it a chore with a counter?
  3. Day 3: open the shop and the market, look at the legendary price tags (the top rig is 500,000 SOL), and compute how many months of free play each one represents.

Then decide. If the answer is "this is a fun game and I want to speed it up", deposit what a video game is worth to you. If the answer is "I want my money back out", do not start.

13. FAQ

Is SolFarmer free to play?

Yes — free sign-up, free starter miner, no wallet needed. Free players earn pennies per month by design; the progression curve starts with the first deposit.

What is the fastest way to level up?

Buy the cheapest miner, place it, merge pairs. Buying, placing and merging each grant XP (~100 XP per starter pair), and level costs scale linearly rather than exponentially.

How does merging work?

Two identical miners at the same level become one of the next level; 16 level-1s become one level-5. Success rate ships as 80% in the client; the exact fee is quoted by the server in the confirmation dialog — read it, the fallback settings contain two different fee models.

Why did my rigs stop earning overnight?

They did not stop — the pending-earnings window is capped at 8 hours by the client. Whatever accumulated past your last claim's 480 minutes is not counted. Claim two or three times a day and you collect everything; the daily check-in, chest and quests take care of the second visit anyway.

Does SolFarmer charge electricity costs?

No. Watts are the power budget of the room a rig sits in (2,000 W in the starter rack, up to 50,000 W in the Industrial Complex). The client ships an electricityCostPerWatt constant but never charges it to your balance. Efficiency per watt matters because of room capacity, not because of a bill — see the energy ladder.

Which rig should I buy first?

Whichever gives the most H/s per SOL at your level: 10.0 for every Common, 11.0–12.8 for Rare, 12.57–13.25 for Epic, up to 15.6 at the very top. The ROI calculator ranks all 48 and models your room's power budget.

What is SSOL and can I withdraw it?

SSOL is the in-game currency added in November 2026. Deposits convert to SSOL one-way; SSOL cannot be withdrawn. Only mining and PvP season rewards produce withdrawable SOL.

What is the minimum withdrawal?

0.1 SOL, on-chain, to a Solana wallet. Verify the address at the confirm step — it is the last point where a mistake is reversible.

What unlocks at level 15?

The Camp: character classes, timed loot expeditions, and offline turn-based PvP with seasonal SOL prizes.

Is SolFarmer a scam?

Not in the classic sense — withdrawals are real and on-chain. It is, however, a pay-to-win game whose economy was restructured in November 2026 so that deposits can no longer leave. Play it free first, deposit only what a game is worth to you, and expect to withdraw rarely.
